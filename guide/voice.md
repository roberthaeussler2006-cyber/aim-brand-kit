# How AIM sounds

Write like the AIM website: short, concrete, confident, a little dry. Swiss-direct, not loud.

## Who we are (from the website manifesto)

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Future bankers, consultants, CFOs | Engineers |
| Users, not theorists | Trainers |
| Sceptical and ambitious | A hype club |
| Swiss-direct | Politely vague |
| Still building | Established |

Positioning in one line: **AI for those who want to lead.** / **KI für die, die führen wollen.**
We care about AI in pitch decks, risk frameworks and client reporting. "Nicht im Tensor."

## Rules

- **Short sentences.** Headlines of two to six words. Often a full stop at the end: "Events." "Drei Dinge, die wir mitnehmen."
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Prompting für Pitch-Decks" beats "Unlock the power of AI".
- **No hype words:** revolutionary, game-changer, unleash, cutting-edge, the future is now, "Revolution", "bahnbrechend".
- **No exclamation marks** in headlines. At most one per design, and only in a call to action if it really helps.
- **No emojis** in designs. In captions: rarely, and never more than one.
- **German: "du"**, never "Sie" (unless the user asks for a formal document for companies).
- **Swiss spelling:** "ss" instead of "ß" (heisst, gross, Strasse). Times as 16.00 Uhr, ranges as "16.00 bis 17.30 Uhr".
  Dates as "Mittwoch, 23. September". Prices as "CHF 40".
- **English:** British spelling (organise, programme), dates as "Wednesday, 23 September", times as "4 pm" or "16:00".
- Keep company and product names exactly as written by the company: PwC, McKinsey, UBS, OpenAI, Claude, ChatGPT.
- **Never invent facts.** Dates, times, rooms, speakers, titles, prices, partner names and numbers come from the user.
  If something is missing, ask or leave it out.
